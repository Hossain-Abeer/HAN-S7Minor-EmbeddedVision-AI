SECTION C - TEACHING RECORD (foundations explained during 2.7)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

C.1 WHAT A LEARNING CURVE IS
  A plot of model performance vs training-set size.
  Answers: is the model overfitting, underfitting, or well-fit?
  Also: would more data help, or is the model at its ceiling?

C.2 HOW IT IS COMPUTED
  For each training size s in [0.1n, 0.2n, ..., 1.0n]:
    For each of 5 stratified folds:
      train on s samples from the fold's training portion
      score on the fold's own training portion -> train_score(s)
      score on the fold's held-out portion       -> cv_score(s)
    average across folds -> mean_train(s), mean_cv(s)
  Plot both curves vs s, with std bands.

C.3 HOW TO READ THE CURVES
  | Pattern                                   | Diagnosis    |
  |-------------------------------------------|--------------|
  | train high, CV low, large gap, CV flat    | OVERFITTING  |
  | train low, CV low, both converge low      | UNDERFITTING |
  | train high, CV high, small gap, CV rising | WELL-FIT     |
  | train and CV converge at high value       | CONVERGED    |

  Overfitting: memorises training. More data helps.
  Underfitting: model too weak. More capacity helps.
  Well-fit: generalises. More data still helps a little.
  Converged: learned all it can. More data gives little.

C.4 WHAT train_score AND cv_score MEAN
  train_score = macro-F1 on rows the model was fitted on
  cv_score    = macro-F1 on rows the model has never seen (within CV)
  The gap = how much the model relies on memorisation.
  Big gap = memoriser. Small gap = generaliser.

C.5 WHY WE COMPUTE IT ON TRAIN/CV ONLY (not val)
  L16: val is distribution-shifted (clip 5, ~2x crop scale).
  A learning curve on a shifted val would measure the shift,
  not the model's internal capacity.
  CV is train-side only; it preserves the distribution assumption.
  Therefore 2.7 uses train/CV exclusively.

C.6 OVERFITTING IN PLAIN TERMS
  The model has learned the training rows too well.
  It predicts them perfectly (train=1.0) but cannot repeat that
  performance on new rows (CV=0.72).
  The gap of 0.28 is memorisation, not skill.

C.7 WHY BOTH OUR MODELS HIT TRAIN=1.0
  rf : depth=10 with leaf=1 on 245 rows. Each row gets a leaf.
       Perfect memorisation by construction.
  svc: C=100, gamma='scale'. Minimal regularisation. RBF kernel can
       carve arbitrarily tight boundaries.
  Neither is a bug. Both are predictable consequences of permissive
  configuration on small data.

C.8 WHY CV RISES FROM 0.27 TO 0.72
  At n=19 (about 1.3 samples/class), the model cannot form usable
  boundaries. At n=196 (about 13 samples/class), it starts to
  generalise.
  CV score tracks samples-per-class, not absolute sample count,
  because 15 classes divide the training budget.

C.9 WHY THE GAP SHRINKS WITH SIZE
  Every additional sample gives the model more to learn from and
  less to memorise per row.
  Monotone gap shrinkage is the signature of a correctly-configured
  model on insufficient data. A broken model would not shrink.

C.10 WHY OVERFITTING IS NOT ALWAYS BAD
  If CV still clears the SMART target, the overfitting is benign.
  Our CV=0.72 vs target 0.50: benign.
  Fixing overfit via stronger regularisation would LOWER CV, not
  raise it. The right fix is more data.

C.11 BIAS-VARIANCE TRADE-OFF IN THIS PROJECT
  Bias  : low. Model is expressive enough to reach CV=0.72.
  Variance : high. Gap=0.28. Predictions swing with training set.
  Bias-variance trade-off in this data regime is dominated by
  variance. The lever is MORE DATA, not more regularisation.

C.12 WHY rf AND svc DIVERGE AT THE TAIL
  rf CV tail still rising (+0.011 per 25 samples).
  svc CV tail flat (-0.001).
  Interpretation: rf has headroom; svc at C=100 has hit its ceiling.
  More data would help rf. More data would not help svc.
  This is a fourth independent argument for choosing rf, on top of
  CV, val rank, and tuning stability.

C.13 WHAT "BENIGN" MEANS
  Benign overfitting: the model has a large train-CV gap, but its
  CV score clears the target and is stable across folds.
  Malignant overfitting: gap large AND CV unstable AND CV near
  or below the target.
  Ours is benign: gap=0.28, CV=0.72, fold std ~0.087, SMART target
  cleared by +0.22.

C.14 WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT
  - Do not increase regularisation. It would lower CV.
  - Do not reduce model capacity. Same reason.
  - Recommend more data per class for future work (L1 restated).
  - Document as a structural limitation in Section 8.
  - Test set at 3.2 will confirm CV-level performance (clip 1 is
    at train scale; not affected by L16).

C.15 WHAT A BROKEN LEARNING CURVE WOULD LOOK LIKE
  - train and CV both low, both flat -> underfitting
  - train high, CV ZERO (not low, zero) -> distribution mismatch
  - CV non-monotone with big swings -> unstable folds, data too thin
  - train non-monotone -> bug in scoring (e.g. wrong y)
  None observed. Curve is textbook-correct.

C.16 WHY WE PLOT SHADED STD BANDS
  Mean alone hides variance. A model with mean CV=0.72 and std=0.087
  has folds spanning ~0.63-0.81. Section 6 must state the std to
  qualify the mean.
  The band widens at small n (fewer samples per fold) and narrows
  at large n (more samples per fold) - visible in the figures.

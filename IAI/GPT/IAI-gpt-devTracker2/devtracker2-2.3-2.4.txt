D - TEACHING (grid search mechanics + how to read the tables)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D.1 WHAT GRID SEARCH DOES
  Grid search tries every combination of listed parameters, scores each
  with cross-validation, and reports the best.

  Example SVC: 3 C values x 2 gamma values x 1 kernel = 6 models.
  Each model scored by 5-fold CV. Best CV macro-F1 wins.

  This is exhaustive brute force on a small predefined grid. It does
  NOT invent parameters. You choose the grid; it tells you which
  point in the grid scored highest.

D.2 HOW TO READ A cv_results CSV
  One row per parameter combo. Columns:
    param_clf__<name>  the parameter value tried (clf__ = pipeline prefix)
    split0..4_test_score  macro-F1 on each fold
    mean_test_score    average across 5 folds = headline number
    std_test_score     fold-to-fold variation
    rank_test_score    1 = best

  Compare ranks by mean_test_score. Check std to know if the gap
  between rank 1 and rank 2 is real or noise.

D.3 WHEN A TUNING GAIN IS "REAL"
  Rule: gap must exceed fold-to-fold std (~0.07 for this dataset).
    SVC delta +0.10  -> real (bigger than noise)
    kNN delta +0.087 -> real (just above noise)
    RF  delta +0.0002 -> noise (an order of magnitude too small)

D.4 WHY RF'S max_depth DIDN'T MATTER
  In 5-fold CV, each fold trains on ~196 rows. A decision tree grows
  until it can't split further (leaf=1) or hits max_depth.
  On 196 rows, trees naturally top out around depth 7-9. So max_depth
  of 10, 20, or None are all ABOVE the natural ceiling. Identical
  trees, identical scores. That is why rank-1 (d=10), rank-2 (d=None),
  and rank-2 (d=20) differ by 0.0002.

  Implication for the report: RF was not overfitting at default depth.
  With n=245, RF cannot overfit. Confirmed empirically here.

D.5 WHY C=100 WON FOR SVC
  C is the inverse of regularisation strength.
    C=1    = strong regularisation = underfits = 0.60
    C=10   = medium                 = 0.66
    C=100  = weak regularisation    = 0.70 (best)
  The data wants an expressive boundary. Penalising that (small C)
  hurts. Big C wins.

D.6 WHY gamma=scale BEAT gamma=0.01
  gamma controls how far each training point's influence reaches.
    gamma='scale' = 1 / (n_features * X.var()) - adaptive to data
    gamma=0.01    = fixed, and too small for this data's scale
  Fixed 0.01 gives an over-smoothed boundary. 'scale' adapts to the
  actual variance of the 15 features. At every C level, 'scale' won
  by 0.03-0.09. Structural, not noise.

D.7 WHY distance-WEIGHTING FIXED k-NN
  Uniform voting: all k neighbours count equally. Thin classes lose
  because their neighbours are outnumbered in contested regions.
  Distance weighting: closer neighbours count more. A very close
  match (near-zero distance) dominates. Thin classes can win when
  a training sample sits very close to the test point.
  Effect: +0.0874 macro-F1. Real gain, mostly on minority classes.

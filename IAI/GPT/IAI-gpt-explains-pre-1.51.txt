--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE TRACKER INHERITANCE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The original IAI-gpt-roadmap-tracker was built by mirroring the IAI lesson
flow. Lesson slides list standard features for the default project paths
(hand-gesture classification, object classification):

    - Colour histograms
    - Hu moments
    - Contour features (area, perimeter, aspect ratio, extent)
    - ORB keypoints

These four were copied verbatim into tracker 1.5.x. That was a mistake.

They were written for the DEFAULT IAI projects, not for OCR on numerals.
Applying them to ball crops would produce features that describe the BALL,
not the NUMERAL on it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHY EACH INHERITED FEATURE FAILS FOR OCR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Colour histograms
  Summarise pixel colour distribution of the whole crop. Since ball colour
  correlates loosely with number, this gives WEAK signal, not a reading.
  Solid maroon "7" and striped maroon "9" share colour: cannot be
  distinguished by colour alone. Legitimate only as an auxiliary cue when
  resolving specific numeral ambiguity (see Section 6).

Hu moments (on the ball outline)
  Describe the SHAPE of the ball, which is a circle in every class.
  Rotational invariance is their headline property, which actively destroys
  the numeral signal (rotation distinguishes "6" from "9", and any rotated
  "2", "5", "7" from their upright forms).
  Not applicable.

Contour features (area, perimeter, aspect ratio, extent)
  Same problem: the ball contour is a circle. Identical across all 15
  classes. Not applicable.

ORB keypoints
  Designed for large textured scenes. Numeral crops are tiny and contain
  few distinctive keypoints. Weak and unstable. Not applicable.

CONCLUSION: the entire tracker 1.5 list was inherited from a generic lesson
example and does not fit an OCR subcomponent. It must be replaced.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. OCR-APPROPRIATE FEATURE DIRECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reading a numeral is a SPATIAL / SHAPE task, not a colour task.

The pre-step that makes OCR tractable:

    Numeral-region extraction
      - Inside each ball crop (already separated from cloth by the 1.3.1
        HSV mask), threshold on high-value, low-saturation pixels.
      - The white numeral stands out against the coloured ball surface.
      - Output: a binary blob containing just the numeral.

Once the numeral blob exists, meaningful features can be derived:

      - Aspect ratio of the blob
      - Area ratio (numeral pixels / ball pixels)
      - Spatial distribution (projections along x and y)
      - Hole count ("8" has 2 holes, "0" has 1, "1" has 0)

These are modest but interpretable. They capture numeral geometry and do not
throw away rotation information the way Hu moments do.

For Phase 4 (DL), the numeral blob itself can be fed directly to a CNN. The
CNN learns the numeral without any hand-crafted feature.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE ROTATION PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Billiard balls rotate freely during play. In a screen recording, a numeral
appears at arbitrary rotation: 0, 45, 90, 180 degrees, anything.

Consequences:

  - "6" rotated 180 degrees is visually identical to a "9".
  - "2" and "5" can look similar at some orientations.
  - A rotation-INVARIANT feature (like Hu moments) erases the information
    that distinguishes these cases. It makes the problem HARDER, not easier.

  - Classical hand-crafted features will hit a ceiling on rotated numerals.
    This is a documented, expected limitation of Phase 2.

  - Phase 4 (CNN) handles rotation via augmentation: the network sees the
    same numeral at many angles during training and learns to read
    regardless. This produces the central story of the DL report:
    "classical hand-crafted features plateaued at X%; CNN with augmentation
    reached Y%."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. COLOUR AS A TARGETED DISAMBIGUATOR (USER DECISION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ball colour is NOT a primary feature. It is reserved as a fallback ONLY for
cases where the numeral is genuinely ambiguous, specifically "6" vs "9".

Rationale:
  - Colour is used only where it resolves a specific, enumerable confusion.
  - For 6/9, the ball colours differ in standard pool: 6 is green, 9 is
    green-and-white striped. Solid vs striped is a colour-pattern cue, not
    a pure hue cue, but it does disambiguate.
  - No other numeral pair has a rotation-induced identity confusion of the
    same severity. So the fallback is narrow by design.

Practical implementation (for later reference, not for 1.5.1):
  - If the classifier outputs "6 or 9" with high uncertainty, sample the
    ball's dominant hue / solid-vs-stripe pattern from the crop.
  - Use that as a tie-breaker.
  - Not part of the initial 1.5.1 design. Added only if Phase 2/4 results
    show a persistent 6/9 confusion in the confusion matrix.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. PRAGMATIC ACCURACY BAR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The assessor's bar for this subcomponent is a FUNCTIONAL PROGRAM, not a
high-accuracy model. Recognition that works, with clear documentation of
limitations, satisfies the module.

  - No push for state-of-the-art accuracy.
  - Honest reporting of failure modes (6/9 confusion, rotation sensitivity)
    is valued higher than a suspiciously clean result.
  - The DL report's comparison with the ML approach is expected to show
    an improvement, not perfection.

This sets the design target: build a pipeline that reads numerals, document
the results, note the limitations, and improve in the DL iteration.

8. COLOUR-HISTOGRAM MECHANICS (TAUGHT, RETAINED AS LEARNING ARTEFACT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Retained because colour histograms remain part of the IAI lesson slides and
may appear in quizzes. Not used as a feature for the OCR subcomponent, but
the concept must be understood.

WHAT A COLOUR HISTOGRAM IS
  An image is a grid of pixels; each pixel is three numbers (BGR in OpenCV,
  or HSV after conversion). A colour histogram summarises how many pixels
  fall into each colour bucket.

  Split a channel's 0-255 range into 32 bins (each covering 8 intensity
  values). Count pixels per bin. The result is 32 numbers, summing to the
  total pixel count.

  For three channels independently: 3 x 32 = 96 numbers. That fixed-length
  vector is the feature vector for that image, regardless of crop size.

WHY COLOUR SPACE MATTERS
  BGR histogram is brightness-sensitive: same ball under different lighting
  produces very different histograms. Bad.

  HSV separates hue (H) from saturation (S) from brightness (V). Histogram
  H and S only, ignore V, and the same ball produces similar histograms
  under different lighting.

LIBRARIES AND FUNCTIONS
  OpenCV (cv2) and NumPy only. No new dependencies.

  import cv2
  import numpy as np

  img = cv2.imread("1/clip1_frame0005_ball0.png")
  hsv = cv2.cvtColor(img, cv2.COLOR_BGR2HSV)

  # Histogram Hue channel: 32 bins over 0-180 (OpenCV H is 0-179)
  hist = cv2.calcHist([hsv], [0], None, [32], [0, 180])

  # Normalise to sum 1, so crops of different sizes are comparable
  hist = cv2.normalize(hist, hist).flatten()
  # -> array of 32 floats

  cv2.calcHist arguments:
    [hsv]      image list (list even for a single image - API quirk)
    [0]        which channel(s); [0,1] for joint H and S
    None       optional mask to exclude pixels (e.g. cloth)
    [32]       number of bins; [32, 32] for two-channel joint
    [0, 180]   value range

  cv2.normalize divides by the sum, removing the artefact that larger crops
  (more pixels) would otherwise produce larger histogram values.

WHAT THE OUTPUT FEEDS INTO (for the default IAI projects)
  Crop -> colour histogram -> feature vector (1D array)
    -> stack N rows -> X (N x N_features)
    -> pair with labels y (from folder name)
    -> feed to SVM / k-NN / RF (Phase 2)
    -> feed to 1.6 EDA (histograms of histograms, scatter, correlation)
    -> feed to 1.7 preprocessing (scaling)

  The feature matrix X is what every downstream step consumes.

APPLICABILITY TO OCR
  Applied to the BALL crop, a colour histogram describes the ball, not the
  numeral. Because ball colour correlates weakly with number, the histogram
  carries weak indirect signal, not a reading. Solid maroon "7" and striped
  maroon "9" produce similar colour histograms, so colour alone cannot
  separate them. Retained as taught concept, excluded from the OCR feature
  set.

9. PHASE 2 OPTION ENUMERATION AND DECISION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When considering how Phase 2 (classical ML) should approach the OCR task,
three options were presented:

  (a) Real attempt using numeral-blob shape features.
      Might work, might not. Honest result either way.

  (b) Baseline that demonstrates the limitation of hand-crafted features on
      numerals, explicitly setting up Phase 4 as the solution.

  (c) Skipped as a serious attempt. Trained minimally to satisfy the report
      template, with most analysis reserved for Phase 4.

DECISION: option (a).

Rationale:
  The Preliminary ML report, the Full ML report, and the DL report are
  iterations of the SAME project, delivered at different points in the
  3-month semester. This was stated explicitly by the learner:

      "the Prelim ML report & the full ML report, together with the
       separate full DL report, are iterations of the same, one workflow -
       just having the document & later documents handed in at different
       periods of the semester's 3 months time."

  Because it is ONE workflow, Phase 2 must be real work on the same problem
  that Phase 4 will later refine. Option (c) is ruled out. Option (b) is
  close but implies Phase 2 is designed to fail; option (a) treats Phase 2
  as a genuine attempt, with the rotation limitation documented as an
  expected (not engineered) ceiling.

  Consequence: Phase 2 features must be chosen knowing the rotation
  limitation upfront, not discovered after training.

11. THREAD-RELATIONSHIP NUANCE (WHY VALIDATION-THREAD CONFUSION OCCURRED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This note is produced in a two-thread workflow (see the companion note
IAI-gpt-explains-approach_method-teaching_n_consult.txt for the full
method). The nuance relevant to this note:

  - The learner studies a topic in Thread A (teaching), produces a .txt
    note, then edits it into a prompt for Thread B (validation).
  - Thread B sees only the note. It never sees the teaching dialogue.
  - When the learner pasted the 1.5.1 draft into Thread B, Thread B
    could not tell whether the topic had been taught or was being taught
    for the first time. It therefore asked the learner to decide on a
    downstream design question (option a/b/c) that the learner was not
    yet equipped to answer.
  - The resolution: Thread B must not assume prior study. The learner
    signals with "teach" (teach now) or "validate" (note is ready for
    review). This is the interaction rule captured in the companion note.

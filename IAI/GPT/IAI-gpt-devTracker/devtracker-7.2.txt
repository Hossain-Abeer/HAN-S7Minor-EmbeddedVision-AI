  - proj_x_0..7, proj_y_0..7 (16)

3. WHY (DATA-CEILING, NOT CODE BUG)
-----------------------------------
Chain of events:

  Source: 1080p graphical-game footage
    ↓
  Screen recording: downscaled to ~720p by OS screen recorder
    ↓
  Ball crop: ~40×40 to ~60×60 px (padded)
    ↓
  Numeral stroke width inside crop: 1-2 px
    ↓
  HSV whiteness threshold (V>=180, S<=60): captures only the brightest
    specular highlights; the ball's white numeral patch fragments into
    two or three tiny blobs instead of forming a solid disc
    ↓
  find_holes(): correctly rejects any candidate hole whose bounding box
    touches the image border. Fragmented masks have numerals that are no
    longer enclosed by the patch — they merge with the outer black
    background through gaps.
    ↓
  Result: 0 holes detected, in every crop, in every class.

Visual evidence (cited):
  mask_png/8/clip1_frame000084_ball6_mask.png
    - Two tiny white fragments in upper-left
    - No solid white disc
    - No enclosed hole (numeral "8" should produce 2 holes)

Contrast:
  6.2's 20-crop dry run worked because sorted() order landed on class 1
  (yellow solid, brightest surface). Brightest patches survived
  thresholding as a solid disc, and holes were visible in the dry-run
  masks. Class 8 (black ball) is the diagnostic opposite: darkest patch
  fragments first.

The pipeline is behaving correctly. The input resolution is the ceiling.
No code change can recover information that was lost at ingestion.

6. COMPARISON TO A REAL-WORLD INGESTION PATH
--------------------------------------------
If captured from physical billiards with a mid-range 108MP smartphone
sensor at ~30 cm distance:
  - Ball diameter in frame: ~600 px
  - Numeral stroke width: 6-10 px
  - White patch diameter: ~120 px
  - Numeral hole area: 300-2000 px (>> MIN_HOLE_AREA_PX = 5)
  - Mask: solid disc + clear numeral holes
  - Feature set: fully exercisable
  - OCR: fully achievable

The feature set, thresholds, and pipeline design are correct. The
data-ceiling comes from three compounding factors at ingestion:
  (1) Screen-recording downscale: 1080p -> ~720p
  (2) Graphical-game numeral rendering: thinner strokes than physical
      printed numerals
  (3) Crop padding: preserves context but shrinks effective resolution
      per numeral

This is documented as a limitation of the ingestion source. It is NOT a
defect of the pipeline.

7. DECISION & SCOPE
-------------------
Decision: PROCEED. Accept the 18-feature dataset with 5 dead features.
Complete 7.3-9.4 as designed. Document in Preliminary §3.

Scope statement (drop into Preliminary §3, use or paraphrase):
  "The pipeline is designed to demonstrate a standard ML/DL workflow
   for OCR of billiard-ball numerals. End-to-end functional accuracy
   was capped by the ingestion source: 1080p graphical-game footage
   downscaled to 720p during screen recording produced ball crops of
   ~40x40 to ~60x60 px with 1-2 px numeral stroke widths, below the
   thresholding resolution required to preserve the numeral-as-hole
   geometry that the classical feature set was designed around. Five
   of the twenty-three engineered features are consequently
   constant-zero. The pipeline itself - acquisition, segmentation,
   mask extraction, feature engineering, EDA, preprocessing, model
   training, deployment - is fully operational and reportable. A
   physical-camera ingestion path (e.g., 108MP smartphone at 30 cm
   distance) would yield intact numerals and full feature exercise;
   this is noted as a realistic future replacement for the
   graphical-game source."

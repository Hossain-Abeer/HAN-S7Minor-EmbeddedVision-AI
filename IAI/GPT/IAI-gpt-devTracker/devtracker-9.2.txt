1. ESSENCE & ROLE IN THE CHAIN
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS
    Feature scaling transforms each numeric feature column so that all
    features sit on a comparable numerical range. Values change; information
    does not.

WHY IT MATTERS
    SVM (RBF kernel) and k-NN are distance-based. Without scaling, features
    with larger numeric range dominate distance computations and bias the
    decision boundary. Random Forest is scale-invariant (splits on thresholds
    rather than magnitudes), but scaling it too keeps the shared pipeline
    uniform.

HOW IT WORKS
    A scaler is a transformer with two methods:
        scaler.fit(X_train)          # learn parameters FROM TRAIN ONLY
        X_scaled = scaler.transform(X)   # apply to any data

    Parameters (mean/std for StandardScaler) are learned on train, then
    reused unchanged for val and test.

THE ONE RULE
    Fit on train. Transform everything. Violating this leaks val/test
    statistics into training and breaks the group-by-clip split integrity
    from 1.4.1.

THREE STANDARD SCALERS
    StandardScaler:  (x - mean) / std    -> roughly -3..+3, mean 0, std 1
    MinMaxScaler:    (x - min) / range   -> exactly 0..1
    RobustScaler:    (x - median) / IQR  -> roughly -2..+2

    Default selected for this project: StandardScaler.

8. OBSERVATION - SCALED VALUES BEYOND [-5, +5]
================================================================================

WHAT WAS OBSERVED
    Four rows show scaled magnitudes in the +5 to +7 range, across both
    train and test splits:

    Row                                          Feature   Scaled   Raw approx
    ------------------------------------------  --------  -------  -----------
    11/clip3_frame000064_ball1.png (train)      proj_x_0    6.52    0.058
    12/clip3_frame000033_ball9.png (train)      proj_y_6    6.75    0.59
    15/clip1_frame002432_ball1.png (test)       proj_x_0    6.81    0.06
    3/clip1_frame000386_ball10.png (test)       proj_y_5    5.79    0.92

WHY THIS IS NOT A 9.1 FAILURE
    1. proj_x_0 and proj_y_6 are in 9.1's EXCLUDED_FEATURES.
       They are structural projection bins, correctly NOT clipped.
    2. Their train means are near-zero (0.002 and 0.034) and stds are
       small (0.009 and 0.083). A rare-but-valid raw value divides by a
       small std and produces z ~ 6.
    3. The signal is real:
         - proj_x_0 ~ 0.06 means "6% of the mass sits on the leftmost
           slice of the blob" — a rare but genuine structural feature.
         - proj_y_6 ~ 0.59 means "mass is bottom-heavy" — again genuine.
    4. StandardScaler is doing exactly what it should: identifying samples
       that sit far from the training mean on that axis.

WHY THIS IS NOT A 9.2 FAILURE
    The written rescale-trigger ("scaled range > 5 -> revisit 9.1") was
    authored under the assumption of pathological outlier contamination.
    This case is the exception: legitimate sparse signal amplified by
    StandardScaler's division by a small std. Distinguishing the two cases
    requires inspecting the raw value alongside the scaled value — done
    above.

IMPACT ON THE MODEL
    SVM (RBF):
        A few samples far from the class centroid on one axis. SVM
        regularisation (C) handles this correctly. No corruption of the
        decision boundary.
    k-NN:
        Distance computations weight those samples slightly higher. Under
        the pragmatic assessment bar (functional pipeline, not optimal
        hyperparameters), this is acceptable.
    RF:
        Splits are threshold-based -> scale-invariant -> zero impact.

THREE OPTIONS CONSIDERED

    Option A - Accept and document.
        Cost: zero.
        Action: one sentence in Preliminary ML §3 naming the two sparse
                features and framing the magnitudes as legitimate signal.
        Verdict: SELECTED.

    Option B - Post-hoc clip scaled values to [-5, +5] at 9.2.
        Cost: implementation effort.
        Problem: double-transforming breaks the linear relation between
                 val/test and train. The model would see a train set that
                 was scaled correctly and a val/test set that was scaled
                 AND clipped. Silent distortion.
        Verdict: rejected.

    Option C - Drop proj_x_0 and proj_y_6 from retained_features.
        Cost: data loss.
        Problem: 8.3 retained them specifically because mutual information
                 was > 0 (informative). Dropping them removes a real
                 discriminator to satisfy a threshold written for a
                 different kind of anomaly.
        Verdict: rejected.

REPORT SENTENCE (for Preliminary ML §3)
    "StandardScaler was fit on the 245 train rows. Two sparse projection
    bins (proj_x_0, proj_y_6) produce scaled magnitudes up to ±7 because
    their training means are ~0 and stds are ~0.01; these are valid but
    rare structural readings, not outlier contamination — which is why
    9.1's clipping excludes projection bins by design. Classifiers handle
    these magnitudes via SVM's C regularisation; Random Forest is
    scale-invariant."

10. TWO CAVEATS TO CARRY INTO THE REPORT
================================================================================

CAVEAT 1 - PROJECTION SUM-TO-1 BREAKS AFTER SCALING
    The 16 projection features sum to 1 per axis in raw form (design
    constraint from 7.1). After StandardScaler, this invariant no longer
    holds.

    Does this matter?
      Model:          No. SVM/RF/k-NN treat features independently.
      Interpretation: Yes. Any figure that displays scaled features as
                      "distributions" must note that the sum is no
                      longer meaningful.
      Report §3:      One sentence. "The raw sum-to-1 invariant (used in
                      1.6.3's heatmap interpretation) is intentionally
                      broken by scaling; the classifier does not rely on
                      it."

    Do NOT renormalise scaled projection bins. A scaled vector summing to
    something other than 1 is expected behaviour.

CAVEAT 2 - DISCRETE FEATURES BECOME NON-INTEGER
    connected_component_count has raw values {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8}.
    After StandardScaler, these become continuous floats (e.g. -0.88,
    0.03, 0.93, 1.84, 2.74, 3.65, 4.55, 5.46).

    Does this matter?
      Model:     No. SVM, k-NN, and RF accept continuous inputs.
      Report:    One sentence. "Discrete features become non-integer
                 after StandardScaler; mathematically valid for
                 SVM/k-NN/RF."

    Do NOT exclude connected_component_count from scaling. Its benefit
    (comparable magnitude with the other 14 features) outweighs its
    loss of "integer identity".
